Data schema, asset taxonomy
and use cases

Built from the asset-class operator sheet and the Rao–Deshpande household. One model has to hold a salaried couple's demat account, a commercial lease, an unlisted ESOP grant, a parent's SCSS and an HUF land parcel without special-casing any of them.

31 Aug 2026
v0.1
The one idea

A holding is never owned by a user. It is owned by an entity — a person, a joint pair, an HUF, a trust, a company — and users get access to entities. Everything else in this schema follows from separating ownership from visibility.

1 · Model at a glance

Read top to bottom: people form a household, the household contains owning entities, entities hold positions, positions are valued and produce cash.

Person ──┬── Membership ──── Household ──── AccessGrant ──┐
         └── EntityRole ──── Entity (individual · joint · HUF · trust · company · minor)
 
Entity ──┬── Source (bank · demat · AA · manual · registrar)
         ├── Holding ──┬── Instrument ──── AssetClass ──── SubType
         │            ├── OwnershipSplit (entity, share %)
         │            ├── Valuation[] (method, as_of, source)
         │            ├── Transaction[]
         │            ├── IncomeStream[] (rent · coupon · dividend · distribution)
         │            ├── Commitment[] (undrawn · exercise cost)
         │            └── Document[]
         ├── Liability ──── LiabilityClass ──── Schedule[] ──── Document[]
         └── Consent ──── Source (scope, purpose, expiry, revoked_at)

4 · Use cases, with the options

Each one is a real fork in the Rao household. The pick is mine; the alternatives are listed because you may weigh them differently.

01 · Manoj's ESOPs are worth ₹1.8 Cr, on paper

Illiquid valuation

Unlisted, priced at a Series D six months ago, exercisable only in a company buyback window. If it sits in the same total as a savings balance, the total is a fiction.

Option A
One estimate field
User types a number. Simple, and immediately unauditable.
Option B · picked
Valuation events + rule
Store the grant (units, strike, vest) and each price event with its source. Value is computed, labelled with its age, and excluded from the liquid bucket.
Option C
Two totals everywhere
Net worth and "net worth excluding unpriced". Honest, but doubles every figure in the product.

02 · Whose ₹20.18 Cr is it?

Attribution

Three properties are jointly held, the HUF has its own PAN, and the parents' assets are theirs alone. A single number hides all of that; six numbers hide the household.

Option A
One household total
Simplest. Wrong at tax time, and wrong if the marriage or the family changes.
Option B · picked
Entity totals that roll up
Every holding sits in one entity; the scope selector adds them. Joint entities split by their declared share for per-person views.
Option C
Contribution-weighted
Split by who actually paid. Truest economically, needs a payment history most people can't reconstruct.

03 · Nirmala should see her own money, and nothing else

Visibility

Ananya administers eight portfolios. Manoj should not see his in-laws' balances. The children shouldn't see anything yet. Everyone should know what everyone else can see.

Option A
Household-wide access
One family, one view. Fails the first time a member wants privacy — and they always do.
Option B · picked
Grants per entity, four levels
none / totals-only / read / manage, each with an expiry, plus a visible "who can see this" row on every holding.
Option C
Per-holding grants
Maximum control, unmanageable at 60 holdings. Keep it for documents only.

04 · An AIF drawdown notice arrives for ₹57 L

Commitments

Committed capital is an obligation with ten working days' notice. It is not a liability, and it is not nothing.

Option A
Record when drawn
Clean books, blind user.
Option B · picked
Commitment ledger + headroom
Undrawn commitments listed with the liquid figure they'd be met from. Net worth untouched.
Option C
Deduct from liquid
Understates cash on hand and confuses the balance-sheet view.

05 · The ICICI feed has been down since 14 August

Staleness

₹21.79 L of the total is seventeen days old. The user has to know that without the number vanishing.

Option A
Hide stale holdings
Net worth silently drops. The worst possible message.
Option B · picked
Show, date, and break the line
Last known value with its as-of, a stale badge on the account, and a gap in the chart rather than interpolation.
Option C
Freeze the whole total
Accurate about uncertainty, useless for the 22 sources that are fine.

06 · Rohan turns 18 in 2034

Life events

Guardian folios must move to him, and the household's view of him has to change from "ward" to "member".

Option A
Automatic on birthday
The registrar's paperwork doesn't happen automatically, so the app would be lying.
Option B · picked
Dated prompt with a checklist
Ninety days before, list what changes: folio transfer, KYC, new login, access grants to review.
Option C
Nothing, user's problem
Defensible, but this is exactly what a household product is for.

07 · If something happens to Ananya

Handover

She is the only person who knows where everything is. That is the single largest risk in this household, and it is a design problem, not a legal one.

Option A
Nominee fields only
What every bank already does, and what nobody can find in a hurry.
Option B · picked
Sealed handover pack
A generated inventory — entities, holdings, contacts, documents — released to a named person on a documented trigger, with every read logged and revocable while she's alive.
Option C
Continuous shared access
Manoj sees everything all the time. Solves the emergency, costs the privacy.

08 · The HUF, the joint pair and two individuals all file separately

Tax views

Four returns, one household, and rental income that has to land against the right PAN.

Option A
Household FY only
One April–March view for everyone. Useless to a CA.
Option B · picked
Per-entity FY pack
Each entity produces its own FY 2026–27 statement: income by head, realised gains with holding periods, interest certificates, TDS. Exportable, not filed.
Option C
Full tax computation
Crosses from information into advice and regulated territory. Not without counsel and a registration.

09 · One employer is 9% of the household's assets and 43% of its salary

Concentration

Manoj's ESOPs, the same company inside two funds, and the salary that pays the EMIs all move together. Saying so is information; saying what to do is not ours to say.

Option A
Say nothing
Safest. Also the reason the household wouldn't need us.
Option B · picked
Look-through exposure, stated flatly
Aggregate every route to the same issuer — direct, fund look-through, ESOP, employment — and show one number with its working. No verb telling them what to do.
Option C
Risk score with a threshold alert
A score implies a recommended level, which is advice wearing a number's clothes.

5 · What multi-portfolio demands of the product

Eight portfolios in one place is not eight copies of one dashboard. These are the things that only exist once you have a household.

01A scope selector, everywhere. Every figure states whose it is. Switching scope never changes the meaning of a column, only its rows.
02Consolidation without double counting. A jointly held flat appears once in the household total and at 50% in each person's view. The rule is stored, not calculated on the fly.
03Look-through across portfolios. Concentration is only visible when Ananya's funds and Manoj's ESOPs are counted against the same issuer.
04Per-member data residency of attention. Nirmala's login opens on her ₹90,00,000, in larger type, with no household total in sight unless she asks.
05Liquidity as a first-class view. With 63% illiquid, "how much is real cash" matters more than the total. It needs windows and exit costs, not a single ratio.
06Contingent and off-balance items. Guarantees, undrawn commitments, exercise costs. Shown, never netted.
07An audit trail per figure. Who entered it, from what source, when. In a household, "where did this number come from" is asked by someone who didn't enter it.
08Succession as a feature, not a form. The inventory a family needs on the worst day is the same inventory the product already holds.
