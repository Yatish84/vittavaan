•Nirmala Rao is not a party, and four things follow from that
The plot is Vasant's self-acquired property, so she has no claim on it during his lifetime and her consent is genuinely not required — not withheld, not waived, simply not part of the law here.

She is, however, a Class I heir. Had he died owning this plot without a will, she would have taken an equal share of it alongside Ananya and Rohan. The gift removes ₹38,40,000 from what would have reached her that way, and it does so irreversibly.

Separately from anything he owns, he owes her maintenance. Giving away half his assets does not reduce that obligation, and his recorded costs already exceed his pension by ₹2,40,000 a year.

And the plot was bought out of deposits that were part of both their old age. The household view will show her the change when it is drafted, not after it is registered — because she is entitled to see it, not because she can stop it.
Notified
•This is irrevocable once registered
A registered gift cannot be taken back because circumstances change. If Vasant wants a right to live on or use the land, or wants it to return if Ananya predeceases him, those have to be conditions in the deed itself.
Decide now
Only Vasant can initiate this. Ananya administers the household, but a transfer out of a member's own assets is authorised by that member alone.

Why this is not a sale

No consideration passes. That single fact changes the tax treatment, the stamp duty, the wording of the deed and what happens to the cost base. Recording it as a ₹1 sale to save trouble would be a false document and would cost Ananya far more when she eventually sells.

Options Vasant has instead
Leave it to her in a will
No stamp duty, no transfer now, revocable · probate later
Gift, reserving a life interest
She owns it, he keeps the right to use it while he lives
Gift outright
Clean, immediate, irrevocable · what he has chosen

These differ in tax, cost and control. Which is right depends on facts we don't hold — an advocate on the panel will take this for ₹9,000.

What a gift between father and daughter costs

Almost nothing in tax, and something in stamp duty. The number that matters most is not on the bill at all — it is the cost base Ananya inherits, which decides her tax whenever she sells.

Income tax
✓Nothing taxable in Ananya's hands
A gift from a lineal ascendant is outside the charge on gifts received. A father giving to his daughter is the clearest case there is.

✓No capital gain for Vasant
A gift is not a transfer for capital gains. He realises nothing, so he pays nothing, even though the plot is worth more than he paid.

•The tax does not disappear, it moves
Ananya inherits Vasant's cost and his holding period. When she sells, her gain is measured from his ₹36,20,000 in 2026, not from today's value. The ₹2,20,000 of appreciation so far becomes taxable in her hands, not his.

The one thing to confirm before filing

The nominal duty for family gifts is written around residential and agricultural property. This is a non-agricultural residential plot with no building on it. Whether the concession reaches it, or whether the registrar treats it as ordinary land at ₹2,30,400, is exactly the kind of question that is settled by asking the advocate before the appointment rather than at the counter. The gap is ₹2,30,200.

What draft 1 said, and why it changed

The first draft reserved Vasant a right of residence. He removed it: there is nothing built on the plot to reside in, and an unused reservation on the title would complicate any sale or loan Ananya wanted later. The advocate noted that a reservation is not the way to protect an elderly donor — a will, or keeping the asset, is.

Three things that make a gift fail
—Not registered · an unregistered gift of land transfers nothing
—Acceptance not recorded during the donor's lifetime
—Possession never handed over, so the gift reads as intention only

All three are addressed in the clauses above. Families lose land to these, decades later, in front of a court.
