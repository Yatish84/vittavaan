The four mechanisms
Nominee, gift, will, inheritance

Three rules decide every gift. Two are about the person, one is about the asset.

One
You may gift to anyone at all

A friend, a neighbour, a trust, a stranger. Nothing in law restricts who may receive a gift. The restriction is on the tax, not the recipient.

Two
Only a defined relative receives it tax free

Everyone else pays income tax on the whole value, at their own slab, once the year's gifts from non-relatives pass ₹50,000. The list of relatives is closed and specific.

Three
You can only gift what you own

Not a jointly held flat, not a pledged one, not a provident fund, not an unvested option, not an account you are merely nominee on. Checked against your ledger →

The asymmetry most families miss

Ananya to her niece is exempt. Her niece to Ananya is taxable. Nothing about the family changed — only which of the two is receiving. An aunt is a relative of her niece; a niece is not a relative of her aunt. Before drafting anything, read the list from the side of the person receiving.

One thing no check can catch

Whether you should still own it. A gift that leaves you short at 80 is legal, registered, tax free and wrong. The ledger can tell you what you are allowed to give away. It cannot tell you what you can afford to.

A nominee is not a beneficiary

A nomination tells a bank, an insurer or a depository who to hand the money to. It does not decide who keeps it. The nominee receives it as a trustee for whoever inherits under the will, or under the law if there is no will, and is obliged to pass it on.

Both mechanisms operate, in that order. The nomination gets the money out of the institution in weeks. The will decides where it settles. When the two disagree, the will wins and the nominee is holding somebody else's money — usually a sibling's, usually without knowing it.

Why it is worth fixingA nomination that matches the will costs nothing and saves the family the argument entirely. A mismatch costs nothing today and everything later.
Where this bites in your fileTwo of Vasant's accounts still name a deceased nominee, and one names a nominee his 2011 will contradicts. Open his will →
One exceptionShares held in demat pass to the nominee as owner under the depository rules, and the courts have not settled this cleanly against the will. Treat it as contested, not as decided.
Choosing between them

If you want it to move now and you are certain, gift it. If you want to decide now and move it later, write a will. If you want it moved cheaply and you do not mind waiting, a will beats a gift on cost every time — no duty, no registration, and the heir gets the asset at its value on the day you die rather than your old cost base.

The one thing a will cannot buy is the sight of it happening.
