Probate

Whether the will must be proved in court before the executor can act depends on where the deceased lived and where the assets are — it is required for some places and not others. With all four institutions accepting the registered will and the nominations, the advocate's view is that it is not needed here. That view is his, recorded and dated.

Five institutions, five different processes

Every one wants the death certificate, a form of its own, and proof that the person asking is entitled to ask. The executor's job is mostly waiting; the platform's job is knowing what each of them needs before they ask.

The word that did not appear once

Succession certificate. Every account had a living, correct nominee, so no institution required a court order to release anything. In 2027 the savings account had no nominee and the fund folios named a man who had died in 2021. Correcting those took two visits and cost nothing, and it is the single reason this took four months rather than two years.

Nominee, then will

Both operated, in that order. The nominee got the money out of the institution quickly; the will decided who keeps it. Here they name the same person, so the second step was invisible.

Where they disagree, the nominee holds the money for whoever the will names, and hands it over. Families who do not know this are the ones who end up in court.

Tax and cost base

Inheriting is not taxable. What you inherit still carries its history.

India has no estate duty and no inheritance tax. Nirmala pays nothing on receiving ₹40,83,000. Three things do follow the assets to her, and they are worth more than most people realise.

✓Nothing taxable on receipt
Inheritance is outside the charge on gifts received, whoever the deceased was. No estate duty exists to pay either.

✓The fund units carry his cost and his clock
