Fourteen capabilities,
and the order they can exist in

Your list is a platform, not a feature set. Some items make the others possible, three of them are regulated activities, and two of them are in direct tension with the promise the brand already made. This map sorts them into layers, names the gate on each one, and proposes what to build in what order.

Four of your fourteen items — recommendations, event-based nudges, credit, and an advisory marketplace — are the parts that make money. They are also the parts that convert Vittavaan from a companion into a seller. The platform works only if the ledger stays independent of the marketplace, visibly and structurally. That is a design decision, not a compliance footnote.

3 · The four hard problems

Not "risks" in the abstract. Each of these decides whether the item ships as described or ships differently.

A blackbox loan is the one thing this brand cannot sell

"Blackbox" and "know what you own" cannot occupy the same product. The capability is right — a household with ₹6.4 Cr of liquid assets and ₹2.2 Cr of annual income should be able to raise credit in a few taps. The framing has to invert: one tap to see what you can borrow against, and every term visible before you agree. Loan against securities, against property, against gold, top-up on an existing facility — priced by partner lenders bidding on your verified position, with the APR, tenure, charges and what happens if you miss a payment on one screen. The magic is that we already hold the collateral picture; the honesty is that nothing is hidden.

Not this
"You're pre-approved for ₹50,00,000. Tap to accept." Terms after the fact.
This
"Three lenders will lend against your listed equity. 9.1% to 10.4%, ₹42 L to ₹58 L, here is each one's margin call rule."

The moment we earn on a transaction, our analysis is no longer neutral

Items 12, 13 and 14 are transaction businesses. Items 2, 3 and 7 are the reason the user trusts us. If the same screen that says "this fund is expensive" also earns a fee when they switch, the sentence is worthless. Three structural answers, in increasing strength: disclose the fee at the point of mention; separate the surfaces so analysis never contains a marketplace call-to-action; or take no transaction fee at all and charge a subscription. My recommendation is the second as a minimum, with the first mandatory and the third worth modelling — a household at this level will pay for a product that is visibly not selling to them.

A financial LinkedIn is a safety question before it is a product question

Publishing verified wealth in India exposes people to extortion, fraud targeting, family pressure and tax scrutiny. The useful core survives without the exposure: a financial resume made of proofs, not amounts — "verified investor since 2016", "AIF Category II participant", "operating income above ₹1 Cr, verified", "12 syndicate investments, 3 exits" — where the counterparty sees a claim we have checked and never the balance behind it. Bands, badges and time, chosen field by field, revocable, with a log of who looked. That version can be public. A leaderboard of net worth cannot.

Talking to the platform is a retrieval problem, not a chat problem

The value is that it can answer "what did we spend on the Bengaluru flat over three years, and what has it returned including rent" — a question no dashboard can anticipate. The rules that make it safe are few and absolute: it answers only from the user's ledger and named public sources; every number it says carries the same as-of stamp the screen would; it can compute and explain but never prescribe; when the ledger cannot answer, it says so and offers to link what's missing. Same four refusals as the rest of the product, and one addition — it never speculates about the future of a holding, even when asked directly.

5 · Order of build

Phase 1The ledger, complete. Tracking, expenses, family. Plus performance analysis, because it needs nothing but transaction history and is the first thing that makes the ledger worth having.Items 1, 6, 9, 2
Phase 2Planning and calculators. Goals funded by real holdings, simulations that write back into the plan. Cheapest layer to build, highest retention, no licence.Items 4, 5
Phase 3Ask, news, and life events. The conversation layer on top of a ledger that can now answer. Public-figure portfolios here, from disclosures only.Items 3, 7, 8, 10
Phase 4Credit and advisory. Both need partners, not licences of our own, and both need the fee wall designed before the first screen.Items 12, 14
Phase 5Asset marketplace and the resume. The hardest two: one is a legal path per asset class, the other is a privacy design problem. Both are better with a large verified user base than without one.Items 13, 11
Standing caveat

Everything on this page about licences, registrations and regulatory gates is my reading as a designer, for sequencing purposes. Which of these activities Vittavaan may perform, and under which registration or partnership, is a question for counsel before any of it is built — the brand bible already says so at §18, and items 3, 7, 12, 13 and 14 are precisely why.
