01

The name

Where it comes from, what it means, and how to write it.

Vittavaan is Sanskrit. It does not describe the product. It describes the person using it.

Etymology

वित्त
vitta
Wealth; property; means. From the root √vid — which carries two senses at once: to find and to know.
+
वान्
-vān
A possessive suffix: one who possesses. Not one who is given, or managed, or advised.

The root is the whole brand. Vid means to find and to know, because in Sanskrit wealth you cannot account for is not really wealth. That is the product thesis stated in a single syllable, roughly three thousand years before anyone built an aggregation API.

Pronunciation

vit-ta-VAANStress on the final syllable. Double t is held, not clipped.
/ʋɪt̪t̪əʋaːn/Use this in voice, video and screen-reader copy.

Writing the name

Do
  • Vittavaan — one word, capital V only.
  • वित्तवान् in Devanagari, with the halant.
  • Leave it untranslated in every language.
  • On first use in long-form, gloss it once: Vittavaan (Sanskrit: one who possesses wealth).
Don't
  • VittaVaan, Vitta Vaan, Vitta-Vaan, VITTAVAAN in body copy.
  • Shorten to "Vitta" or "VV" — the suffix is the meaning.
  • Add "app", "Inc." or "Technologies" inside the name.
  • Possessive as "Vittavaan's app" — it's just Vittavaan.

All-caps VITTAVAAN is permitted in one place only: the wordmark itself, which is drawn, not typed.

02

Positioning

The sentence every other decision has to answer to.

The category problem

Personal finance software splits into two camps, and both fail the same way. Trackers record what already happened and leave you to interpret it. Advisors interpret it and leave you dependent on the interpretation. One gives you data without understanding; the other gives you understanding without ownership.

Vittavaan is a third thing: a companion. It sits beside you, shows you the whole picture, and explains it until you no longer need it explained. Success is measured by how competent the user becomes, not by how often they open the app.

Positioning statement

For people building wealth across scattered accounts, Vittavaan is a financial companion that brings everything they own into one clear picture and explains it in plain language — unlike trackers that only record and advisors that only prescribe, because the point is for you to understand your money, not to depend on someone who does.

Brand promise

Know what you own.

Four words, plain verbs, second person, active. It states the functional benefit (aggregation) and the deeper one (ownership, in the sense the name already carries) without claiming a return or implying advice — which keeps it clean under the constraints in §18.

Approved alternates

Use only where the primary doesn't fit the space or the context. Never invent new ones locally.

See the whole of it.Aggregation-led contexts; onboarding.
Everything you own, in one line.Net-worth and dashboard features.
Clarity, compounded.Long-term planning and wealth features. Use sparingly — it edges toward return language.

Pillars

Every feature should trace to one of these. A feature that traces to none is somebody else's product.

PillarWhat it commits us to
WholenessOne picture of everything owned and owed — bank, equity, mutual funds, EPF/NPS, property, gold, insurance, liabilities. Partial pictures are the problem we exist to solve.
PlainnessAny figure on screen can be explained in one sentence a non-specialist understands. If it can't, it doesn't ship.
ComposureThe product is calmest when markets aren't. We never manufacture urgency to drive engagement.
TransferWe explain the reasoning, not just the result, so the user's judgement improves over time.
03

Audience

Who we are speaking to, and who we are not.

One primary audience. Two secondary. Everyone else is a nice surprise, not a design input.

Primary — the scattered accumulator

Has assets in six to twelve places and no single view of them. Financially literate but not a specialist. Earns well enough that the money is now complicated, not well enough to have someone manage it. Feels a low, persistent hum of not-quite-knowing where they stand. Their job to be done: "Show me the whole thing, and tell me whether I'm alright."

Secondary — the household CFO

Manages money for a family or a parent as well as themselves. Needs multiple portfolios, shared visibility, and a clean handover story. Cares about documentation more than optimisation.

Secondary — the new earner

Three to five years in. Fewer assets, more questions. Comes for the tracking, stays for the explanation. This is where the Transfer pillar earns its keep, and where tone matters most: never condescend.

Not our audience

Active traders wanting tick data and execution. Institutions. Anyone whose primary question is "what should I buy right now" — that is a question §18 prevents us from answering, and designing for it would corrupt the whole product.

05

Voice & tone

One voice. Tone moves with the user's situation, never with ours.

Four rules

01Plain over polished. "You spent ₹4,200 more than last month," not "expenditure exhibited an upward variance."
02Explain, never predict. We say why a number moved. We do not say where it will go. This is a legal boundary as well as a stylistic one — see §18.
03Steady in a fall. Losses get the same typographic and verbal register as gains. No exclamation marks, no apology, no reassurance we can't back.
04Second person, active voice. "Link your account," not "Account linkage may be initiated."

Tone by moment

MomentTone
OnboardingWelcoming, brief, zero jargon. Earn one step at a time.
Portfolio upFlat and factual. Never congratulatory — the market did it, not the user.
Portfolio downFlat and factual, with context. Same register as above. This symmetry is the brand.
ErrorSay what happened and the one thing that fixes it. No apology, no vagueness.
Empty stateAn invitation, not a shrug. Name the action.
Security & consentSlowest, plainest register in the product. Short sentences. No persuasion.

Written examples

Write this
  • "Your net worth is ₹42,18,600. That's ₹1,12,400 lower than last month, mostly from equity."
  • "We couldn't reach HDFC Bank. Your balance is from 14 August. Retry now."
  • "Nothing here yet. Link an account to see it in your net worth."
  • "This assumes 6% inflation. Change it to see the difference."
  • "Two funds hold the same 14 stocks. That's more concentration than it looks."
Not this
  • "Wow! You're crushing it 🚀 Net worth up big this month!"
  • "Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later."
  • "No data available."
  • "Our proprietary engine projects ₹2.4 Cr by 2041."
  • "You should switch out of these funds."

Words we don't use

guaranteedrisk-freeassured returns bestshould buy / should sellrecommend advicemultibaggerdon't miss out limited timesmash / crush / beast modeeffortless wealth

Words we do use

you ownas ofbecause this assumeshere's how it's calculated roughlywe don't know yetworth a look

"We don't know yet" is a brand asset. Almost no financial product will say it, and saying it is the fastest way to be believed about everything else.

06

Mark anatomy

What the mark is made of, and the one property that governs every lockup.

The ascent mark

Three readings, one form

A checkVerified, reconciled, accounted for. The settled-ledger reading.
A VThe initial. In the primary lockup it is the first letter of the wordmark.
An ascentThe gold arc leaves the base of the V and rises past it — growth that starts from what you already hold.

The governing rule

In the primary horizontal lockup, the green mark substitutes for the letter V. The typeset word beside it reads ITTAVAAN. This is elegant and it is also fragile: the moment the mark is moved, scaled independently, or separated, the wordmark loses a letter.

Therefore: any lockup in which the mark is not immediately left of the word must use the full wordmark, with its own typeset V. Both versions are supplied. Never separate the mark from the primary lockup and leave the word as-is.

Primary — mark serves as the V
Full wordmark — typeset V, for all other uses

Construction notes

Arc terminalThe gold arrowhead is the only sharp point in the identity. It always points up and to the right. Never mirror it.
OverlapThe gold arc passes behind the green V at the base and in front at the crossing. Preserve this z-order in every rebuild.
Letterform AThe wordmark's A is drawn without a crossbar (Λ). This is a wordmark customisation, not a typeface feature. Never reproduce it in body copy.
TrackingRoughly 0.34 em. The wordmark is drawn artwork — never re-typed, never re-tracked.

Clear space & sizing

09

Misuse

These are not hypothetical. Each one has happened to a mark like this.

Never do any of these
01Don't stretch or condense. Scale proportionally, always.
02Don't recolour. The green and gold are fixed. No gradients, no brand-adjacent substitutes, no seasonal palettes.
03Don't rotate or mirror. The arc points up and to the right. That is the whole idea.
04Don't add effects. No drop shadow, bevel, glow, outline or 3D extrude.
05Don't re-type the wordmark. It is drawn artwork. Setting "VITTAVAAN" in a similar face is not the wordmark.
06Don't separate the mark from the primary lockup and leave the word reading ITTAVAAN. Use the stacked lockup. See §06.
07Don't put it in a box it wasn't given — no added circles, rounded rectangles or containers, except the specified app icon.
08Don't crowd it. Clear space is a rule, not a suggestion.
09Don't lock it to other logos. For partnerships, separate with a hairline rule at 1 V, both marks at equal cap height.
10Don't use it as a bullet, pattern or texture. The mark is a signature, not a decoration.

The gold trap

Ascent Gold measures 2.13:1 against Paper. It fails every accessibility threshold as text. It is a graphic colour — arcs, rules, the mark, accents on dark grounds — and it must never be used for body copy, labels, links or numbers on a light background.

Where a gold-feeling text colour is needed on light, use Bronze #7E6329 (5.20:1). On Deep Vault, gold is safe at 7.27:1 and should be used freely.

Semantic colours

This is the sharpest colour problem the brand has. Our primary colour is green, and in finance green already means gain. If we let them touch, every screen becomes ambiguous: is that green because it went up, or because it's the brand?

The wall: Vault Green is structural — grounds, headers, the mark, navigation. It never encodes a value. Data states use their own set, which appears nowhere in brand furniture.

Never colour alone

Roughly one in twelve men has a red–green colour vision deficiency, which is precisely the axis our gain/loss pair sits on. Every value-bearing colour must be doubled by a sign and a glyph.

HDFC Bank▲ +₹18,420  +2.14%
Equity — direct▼ −₹1,04,180  −3.62%

Arrow, sign and colour all carry the same message. Remove any two and it still reads.

Proportion

Across any surface, aim for roughly 60% neutral (Paper, Mist, Ink), 30% Vault Green family, 8% semantic, 2% Ascent Gold. Gold is the rarest thing on the page. That is what makes it read as gold rather than as yellow.

Rules

02Jost never below 18px. Its light weights collapse at text sizes. Below 18px, Manrope.

05Never fake the Λ. The crossbar-less A belongs to the wordmark only.
06Minimum body size 15px on mobile, 16px on web. This audience skews older than the category average.

Numbers & currency

The most-read element in the product. It gets its own standard.

A finance app is mostly a typesetting problem wearing a fintech costume. Get the figures right and half the trust is earned before anyone reads a word.

Grouping

ContextFormat
Default (India)₹12,34,567.89 — Indian grouping, 2-2-3
Non-INR amounts$1,234,567.89 — international grouping, always with currency code on first use: USD $1,234,567.89
Symbol spacing₹1,200 — no space. Never ₹ 1,200 or 1,200₹
Negative−₹1,04,180 — true minus (U+2212), sign before symbol. Never parentheses, never a hyphen.
Zero₹0 — never "-", "nil" or blank

Lakh and crore

Abbreviate only in constrained display — chart axes, sparkline labels, dense list rows, notifications. Never in statements, tax documents, transaction detail, confirmation screens, or anywhere a user might transcribe the figure.

Units are L and Cr — capital, unpunctuated. Not "lac", "lakhs", "crores", "K" or "M" in an INR context.

Alignment

Right-align every column of figures. Align on the decimal. Keep the currency symbol left-aligned in wide tables so the eye reads a clean numeric edge.

Precision & honesty

01Two decimals for percentages (+2.14%), none for rupee display over ₹1,000, two for anything under.
02Always stamp the time. Every figure carries an "as of" — As of 27 Aug 2026, 3:42 PM IST. Stale data is labelled stale, never silently shown.
03Never round up a loss or round down a gain. Round half to even, consistently, in both directions.
04Estimates are marked. If a value is derived, interpolated or user-entered rather than fetched, say so next to it.
05Dates: 27 Aug 2026. Never 27/08/26 — ambiguous across the user base. Financial year as FY 2026–27.

Data visualisation

Charts are copy. They make claims, and the claims have to be true.

Categorical series

For asset classes and allocations, use the ordered sequence below. Take colours in order; never re-order to make a chart prettier, because the same asset class must keep the same colour across every screen.

Series 1#1A3A26
Series 2#3A6B4C
Series 3#7E6329
Series 4#C7A664
Series 5#6B7075
Series 6#A8B0A9

This is a sequential green-to-gold ramp, not a rainbow. It stays legible in greyscale and in both common colour-blindness simulations.

Rules

01Y-axis starts at zero for any bar chart. For line charts of portfolio value, a non-zero baseline is permitted but must be labelled at the axis.
02Label directly. Prefer a label at the end of a line over a legend the eye has to travel to.
03No pie charts beyond five slices, and never for time series. Prefer a horizontal bar or a single stacked bar for allocation.
04No 3D, no shadows, no gradients under lines. The gradient-fill area chart is the visual cliché of this category; we don't use it.
05Gridlines in Mist, hairline, never darker than the lightest data mark.
06Show the gap in the data. If a feed was down, break the line. Never interpolate silently across missing days.
07Every chart states its period and its "as of" in a mono label beneath it.

Iconography

One system, drawn to the same geometry as the mark.

Grid24 × 24, 2 px live-area padding.
Stroke1.75 px, round cap, round join. Never mixed weights in one set.
Corner radius2 px on outer forms — echoes the mark's softened terminals without mimicking them.
FillOutline by default. Solid fill reserved for the selected state in navigation.
ColourInherits text colour. Icons never carry brand colour on their own.
AngleDiagonals at 45° or at the arc's 32°. No other angles.

Never use the mark's arrowhead inside an icon, and never build an icon that reads as a small V. The mark must stay the only V-shape in the interface, or it stops signing anything.

Recommended base set: Lucide, restyled to 1.75 px stroke. Draw custom icons only for concepts no general set covers — EPF, NPS, HUF, demat, SIP.

Imagery

What we show, and the entire genre we refuse.

Yes
  • Real people in ordinary Indian domestic and working settings, unposed, mid-task.
  • Natural light, slightly warm, shallow depth of field.
  • Documents, ledgers, hands, tables — the physical texture of managing money.
  • Wide crops with room for the subject to sit off-centre.
  • Age range genuinely spanning 25 to 65.
No
  • Trading floors, candlestick charts on monitors, bull statues, rockets, upward-arrow stock photography.
  • Coins, currency stacks, piggy banks, gold bars, money raining.
  • People cheering at laptops.
  • Skylines and glass towers — we are not a bank.
  • AI-generated people. Ever.

Treatment

Images sit at 2px radius, never fully rounded. Where text must overlay an image, use a Deep Vault scrim at 55% minimum. No duotone, no green wash — the palette lives around the photograph, not on it.

Motion

Motion carries meaning here, so it is deliberately scarce.

In a product about money, every animation is a claim about importance. Most things are not that important.

PropertyValue
Standard duration180 ms
Entrance / page260 ms
Easing, defaultcubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)
Easing, exitcubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1)
Distance≤ 8 px translate

Principles

01Nothing bounces. No spring overshoot anywhere in the product. Money doesn't bounce.
02Figures count up only on first load of a screen, over 400 ms, and never on a loss. Animating a number downward is cruel and reads as a slot machine.
03Loading uses the arc, drawn once left to right — the mark's own gesture, at 900 ms, no loop-spin.
04No confetti, no haptic celebration, no streaks. Engagement mechanics borrowed from games have no place in someone's net worth.
05Honour prefers-reduced-motion across every surface, including marketing.

18

Trust language

The words that keep the brand honest — and out of trouble.

Read this first

This section is drafting guidance for writers, not legal advice, and it is not a compliance opinion. India's rules for financial platforms sit across SEBI, the RBI, the Account Aggregator framework and the DPDP Act, and what applies to Vittavaan depends on exactly which activities it performs and which registrations it holds. Every line below must be reviewed and signed off by your counsel before it appears in front of a user.

The core distinction

There is a bright line between information and advice. Information describes what is; advice tells someone what to do. Unless and until Vittavaan holds the relevant registration, all product copy must stay firmly on the information side — and the brand is better for it, because §02 already committed us to explaining rather than prescribing.

Information
  • "These two funds hold 14 of the same stocks."
  • "This fund's expense ratio is 1.8%. The category median is 1.1%."
  • "You have ₹6.2 L in cash earning 3%."
  • "Here's how this figure is calculated."
Advice
  • "You should diversify out of these."
  • "Switch to a cheaper fund."
  • "Move this into a liquid fund."
  • "Our model suggests you'll reach ₹2 Cr."

Claims discipline

01Never state or imply a guaranteed, assured or risk-free return.
02Never present a projection without its assumptions visible on the same screen, and never in a way that could be read as a promise.
03Past performance statements always carry the standard caveat, in readable type — not 8px grey.
04Never use "best", "top" or "recommended" for a third-party product.
05Disclose any commercial relationship at the point of mention, not in a footer.
06Never imply endorsement by, or registration with, a regulator we are not registered with.

Talking about data and security

This is where the calmest register in §05 applies. Describe what happens to the user's data in short, concrete sentences: what is collected, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and how to revoke. Never sell security as a feature with a shield icon and the word "bank-grade" — say the specific true thing instead.

Consent screens are the one place where brevity loses to clarity. If a sentence needs to be longer to be unambiguous, make it longer.
